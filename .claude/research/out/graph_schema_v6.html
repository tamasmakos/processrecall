The loop, and where it is open

Tensor Brain says a symbol earns its place by being read back: bottom-up labelling writes the index, top-down recall re-activates the state from it. Two of the three layers close that loop. The semantic layer does not — its two modules have no caller in the package, so every entity the episodic rows already carry is written and never read.

Two readings, and the prototype does not settle between them. Either reciprocal rank is the wrong fusion for lists this unequal — on_entity answers 39% of positions at 5.5% and still gets a full vote — or the entity-conditioned traversals are genuinely weak while every touch resolves to a file rather than a symbol. Why this is worth doing at all

The strongest argument is not in the fixture, it is in the live store: 6908 steps, of which 6908 are neutral — every single one. The same holds for sequences: outcome neutral 395, process type Unknown 395. Outcome, cost and refusal are not sparse here; they are absent. Edge weighting by clean prompts (v5's E2, adopted in design 3.4) has nothing to weight, the usually_refused pitfall has no possible source, and every transition condition is process_type=Unknown. Those four fields are exactly what telemetry carries and the post-action hook cannot.

One pack, three layers

The shipped pack is 19 concepts and 3 predicates, and every one of them describes the procedural layer. The extension keeps all of it and adds what the other two layers need: SEON's performed side (SPO) for the episodic layer, its code artifacts for the semantic layer, and the predicates that cross between them. The alignment that matters most is free: SPO already distinguishes a PerformedActivity from the ActivityDefinition it conforms to — which is exactly the episodic → procedural fold this memory performs.
